Overview
Over time, consistently high blood glucose can damage blood vessels and nerves throughout the body, increasing the risk of both chronic and acute complications. Managing blood sugar, blood pressure, and cholesterol—along with routine medical checkups—can prevent or delay most diabetes complications.

Major long-term complications
- Heart disease and stroke: People with diabetes are at significantly higher risk of cardiovascular disease, heart attack, and stroke. Controlling blood pressure (the American Diabetes Association recommends a target below 130/80 mm Hg for most adults with diabetes), cholesterol, and blood glucose, plus avoiding smoking, greatly reduces this risk.
- Kidney disease (chronic kidney disease / nephropathy): High blood sugar and high blood pressure can damage the tiny blood vessels in the kidneys, eventually leading to kidney failure. Annual urine albumin and blood creatinine (eGFR) tests detect early kidney changes.
- Eye damage (retinopathy, cataracts, and glaucoma): Diabetes can damage the blood vessels of the retina and is a leading cause of preventable blindness. Regular dilated eye exams detect problems early when treatment works best.
- Nerve damage (neuropathy): Excess glucose can injure nerve fibers, most commonly in the legs and feet, causing numbness, tingling, burning pain, or loss of sensation. Autonomic neuropathy can also affect digestion, bladder control, and heart rate.
- Foot problems: Nerve damage and poor blood circulation increase the risk of unnoticed foot injuries, slow-healing ulcers, severe infections, and lower-limb amputations. Daily foot checks and proper footwear are essential.
- Oral, skin, and hearing problems: High blood sugar increases the risk of gum disease, skin infections, and hearing loss.

Acute (short-term) complications
- Hypoglycemia (low blood sugar): Blood glucose dropping below 70 mg/dL, which requires immediate treatment with fast-acting carbohydrates.
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA): A life-threatening emergency caused by a severe lack of insulin and buildup of ketones in the blood.

Protecting yourself
Work with your health care team to stay on top of the diabetes ABCs (A1C, Blood pressure, Cholesterol, and stopping Smoking), take prescribed medications consistently, stay active, eat balanced meals, and keep up with yearly eye, kidney, foot, and dental exams.
