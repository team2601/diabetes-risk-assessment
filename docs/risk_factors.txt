Key points
- Different types of diabetes have different risk factors.
- Some risk factors, such as age and family history, cannot be changed, while others—such as weight, physical activity, diet, and blood pressure—can be improved through lifestyle changes.

Risk factors for type 1 diabetes
Risk factors for type 1 diabetes are not as well understood as those for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes. Known risk factors include:
- Family history: Having a parent, brother, or sister with type 1 diabetes.
- Age: Type 1 diabetes can develop at any age, though it is more commonly diagnosed in children, teens, or young adults.

Risk factors for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes
You are at higher risk for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes if you have one or more of the following risk factors.

Modifiable risk factors (can be changed):
- Overweight or obesity
- Physical inactivity (being active fewer than 3 times a week)
- Unhealthy diet high in added sugars, refined carbohydrates, and unhealthy fats
- High blood pressure (hypertension)
- Smoking or tobacco use

Non-modifiable risk factors (cannot be changed):
- Age 45 or older
- Family history of diabetes (having a parent, brother, or sister with type 2 diabetes)
- History of gestational diabetes (diabetes during pregnancy) or giving birth to a baby weighing more than 9 pounds
- Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)
- Certain racial and ethnic backgrounds, including African American, Hispanic or Latino, American Indian, Alaska Native, and some Pacific Islander and Asian American populations

Risk factors for gestational diabetes
You are at risk for gestational diabetes if you:
- Had gestational diabetes during a previous pregnancy
- Have given birth to a baby weighing more than 9 pounds
- Are overweight or have obesity
- Are older than 25 years of age
- Have a family history of type 2 diabetes
- Have a hormone disorder called polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)

Knowing your risk factors helps you and your health care provider decide when to screen for prediabetes or diabetes and what preventive steps to prioritize.
