What is hypoglycemia?
Hypoglycemia (low blood glucose) occurs when blood sugar drops below 70 mg/dL. Severe hypoglycemia occurs when blood glucose drops below 54 mg/dL or when a person becomes unable to treat themselves and needs assistance from someone else. Hypoglycemia is most common in people taking insulin or certain diabetes pills (such as sulfonylureas).

Common signs and symptoms of low blood sugar
Symptoms can appear quickly and vary from person to person:
- Feeling shaky or jittery
- Sweating, chills, or clamminess
- Fast heartbeat
- Dizziness, lightheadedness, or headache
- Hunger or nausea
- Irritability, nervousness, or sudden mood changes
- Confusion, difficulty concentrating, or blurred vision
- Weakness, fatigue, or tingling around the lips or tongue

Hypoglycemia unawareness
Some people—especially those who have had diabetes for many years or experience frequent lows—may stop feeling early warning symptoms. Regular glucose checks or a continuous glucose monitor (CGM) with low-glucose alarms are critical for safety.

How to treat low blood sugar: The 15-15 Rule
If your blood sugar is between 55 and 69 mg/dL (below 70 mg/dL) and you are awake and able to swallow safely:
1. Consume 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrates to raise blood sugar quickly.
2. Wait 15 minutes, then recheck your blood glucose.
3. If your blood sugar is still below 70 mg/dL, consume another 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrates and recheck after 15 minutes.
4. Once blood sugar is back to at least 70 mg/dL, eat a balanced meal or snack with complex carbs and protein if your next meal is more than an hour away.

Examples of 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrates:
- 3 to 4 glucose tablets or 1 tube of glucose gel
- 4 ounces (1/2 cup) of regular fruit juice or regular (non-diet) soda
- 1 tablespoon of sugar, honey, or corn syrup
Avoid chocolate, ice cream, or high-fat foods for initial treatment because fat slows down sugar absorption.

Treating severe hypoglycemia
If blood sugar drops below 54 mg/dL or the person is confused, unconscious, having a seizure, or unable to swallow, do not give food or drink by mouth. Administer emergency glucagon (nasal spray or injection) if available and call emergency medical services immediately.
