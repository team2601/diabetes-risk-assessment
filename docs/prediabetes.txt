Prediabetes - Your Chance to Prevent Type 2 Diabetes
https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/prevention-type-2/prediabetes-prevent-type-2.html
Key points
- Prediabetes is a serious health condition where blood sugar levels are higher than normal, but not high enough yet to be diagnosed as type 2 diabetes.
- More than 2 in 5 adults in the United States have prediabetes, and most people do not know they have it because it rarely causes clear symptoms.
- Prediabetes increases the risk of developing type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and stroke, but it can often be reversed through lifestyle changes.

What causes prediabetes?
Insulin is a hormone made by the pancreas that acts like a key to let blood sugar into cells for use as energy. With prediabetes, the cells in the body do not respond normally to insulin, a condition known as insulin resistance. The pancreas makes more insulin to try to get cells to respond, and eventually it cannot keep up. Blood sugar rises, setting the stage for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes.

Signs and testing for prediabetes
You can have prediabetes for years without clear symptoms, so it often goes undetected until serious health problems such as type 2 diabetes appear. Talk to a health care provider about getting your blood sugar tested if you have risk factors such as being overweight, being 45 years or older, having a parent or sibling with type 2 diabetes, being physically active less than 3 times a week, or having a history of gestational diabetes.
Prediabetes is diagnosed by blood tests:
- HbA1c test: 5.7% to 6.4%
- Fasting plasma glucose: 100 to 125 mg/dL
- Oral glucose tolerance test (2-hour): 140 to 199 mg/dL

How to prevent or reverse prediabetes
If you have prediabetes, losing a modest amount of weight and getting regular physical activity can significantly lower your risk of developing type 2 diabetes.
- Losing 5% to 7% of your body weight (10 to 14 pounds for a 200-pound person) makes a meaningful difference.
- Getting at least 150 minutes of moderate physical activity per week, such as brisk walking for 30 minutes 5 days a week, improves how the body uses insulin.
- Following a structured lifestyle change program, such as the CDC-recognized National Diabetes Prevention Program (National DPP), can cut the risk of developing type 2 diabetes by up to 58% overall (and by 71% for people over age 60).
