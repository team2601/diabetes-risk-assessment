Key points
- Managing diabetes day to day takes steady habits, regular monitoring, and ongoing support from your health care team.
- Small, consistent daily actions around food, movement, stress, sleep, and medical care protect your long-term health.

Daily habits for managing diabetes
- Cook and plan meals at home: Preparing meals yourself makes it easier to control portions, sodium, added sugars, and carbohydrates. Be cautious of packaged snacks marketed as "diabetes-friendly" or "sugar-free," as they can still contain significant carbohydrates, calories, or unhealthy fats.
- Stay active every day: Build movement into your routine—such as short walks after meals, taking the stairs, gardening, or scheduled workouts—to help regulate blood sugar and boost energy.
- Monitor your health indicators: Check your blood sugar as recommended by your doctor and track your diabetes ABCs (A1C, Blood pressure, Cholesterol, and not Smoking).
- Manage stress: Ongoing stress releases hormones that can raise blood sugar and make self-care harder. Practice stress-reduction techniques such as deep breathing, walking, journaling, or talking with supportive friends or a counselor.
- Prioritize quality sleep: Poor or insufficient sleep can increase insulin resistance and make blood sugar harder to control. Aim for a consistent sleep schedule in a dark, quiet, cool bedroom (around 65 degrees Fahrenheit) and avoid screens before bedtime.

Routine medical care and self-management support
- Keep regular appointments with your primary care provider, eye doctor (annual dilated eye exam), dentist, and foot specialist when needed.
- Check your feet daily for cuts, redness, swelling, or blisters.
- Enroll in Diabetes Self-Management Education and Support (DSMES) services. DSMES connects you with certified diabetes care and education specialists who help you build practical skills for eating well, staying active, monitoring blood sugar, taking medications safely, solving daily problems, and coping with the emotional side of diabetes.
