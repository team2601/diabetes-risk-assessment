Key points
- Meal planning helps keep your blood sugar in your target range while ensuring you get balanced nutrition.
- Carbohydrates raise blood sugar faster and higher than protein or fat, so balancing carbohydrate portions with fiber and lean protein is essential.

Understanding carbohydrates, fiber, and protein
The three main types of carbohydrates in food are starches, sugars, and fiber. Starches and sugars raise blood sugar levels, whereas dietary fiber does not raise blood sugar and helps slow digestion. Pairing whole, high-fiber carbohydrates with lean protein and healthy fats helps prevent sharp blood sugar spikes.

The Diabetes Plate Method
The Diabetes Plate Method is a simple, visual way to create balanced meals without counting calories or weighing food. Start with a 9-inch dinner plate:
- Fill half (1/2) of your plate with non-starchy vegetables, such as broccoli, spinach, salad greens, green beans, peppers, carrots, or tomatoes.
- Fill one quarter (1/4) of your plate with lean protein, such as chicken, turkey, fish, eggs, tofu, beans, or lean cuts of beef or pork.
- Fill one quarter (1/4) of your plate with carbohydrate foods, such as brown rice, whole-grain pasta, whole-wheat bread, oats, potatoes, corn, fruit, or low-fat yogurt.
- Choose water or a zero-calorie drink instead of sugary sodas, sweet tea, or fruit juice.

Portion size versus serving size
- A serving size is the standardized amount listed on a Nutrition Facts label.
- A portion size is the amount of food you actually choose to eat at one time.
When you do not have measuring cups available, use your hand as a quick guide:
- 3 ounces of meat, fish, or poultry is about the size of your palm.
- 1 ounce of cheese is about the size of your thumb.
- 1 cup of vegetables or fruit is about the size of a closed fist.
- 1/2 cup of cooked grains or pasta is about the amount that fits in a cupped hand.

Practical tips for daily eating
- Eat meals at regular times to help keep blood sugar steady.
- Limit ultra-processed snacks, refined white bread, pastries, and sugar-sweetened beverages.
- Read Nutrition Facts labels for total carbohydrates and added sugars.
