Key points
- Checking your blood sugar regularly helps you see how food, physical activity, stress, illness, and medicines affect your glucose levels.
- Keeping blood sugar within your target range lowers the risk of short-term emergencies and long-term complications.

Typical blood sugar targets
For many nonpregnant adults with diabetes, general blood sugar targets are:
- Before a meal: 80 to 130 mg/dL
- Two hours after the start of a meal: Less than 180 mg/dL
Targets can differ based on age, pregnancy, and other health conditions, so ask your health care provider what range is right for you. For continuous glucose monitor (CGM) users, many guidelines also recommend spending at least 70% of the day in target range (Time in Range).

How to check your blood sugar
- Blood glucose meter (fingerstick): Uses a small drop of blood from your fingertip on a test strip to show your glucose level at that exact moment.
- Continuous glucose monitor (CGM): Uses a small sensor placed under the skin to measure glucose continuously every few minutes and display trends and alerts for highs or lows.

Common times to check blood sugar
- When you first wake up, before eating or drinking (fasting)
- Before a meal
- Two hours after a meal
- At bedtime
- Before and after physical activity, or whenever you feel symptoms of low or high blood sugar

When to check for ketones
If you are sick or your blood sugar is 240 mg/dL or higher, check for ketones using urine test strips or a blood ketone meter. High blood sugar with ketones can signal diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), a serious medical emergency. Seek medical care immediately if ketones are moderate to high, if blood sugar stays at or above 300 mg/dL, or if you have vomiting or trouble breathing.
