What is Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)?
Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is a serious, potentially life-threatening emergency that happens when the body does not have enough insulin to move glucose into cells for energy. Instead, the liver breaks down fat rapidly for fuel, producing acidic chemicals called ketones. When ketones build up to dangerous levels in the blood, they make the blood acidic. DKA most commonly occurs in type 1 diabetes, but it can also happen in type 2 diabetes.

Common causes and triggers
- Missing insulin doses or an insulin pump malfunction
- Illness, infection (such as pneumonia or a urinary tract infection), or physical stress
- Newly developing, undiagnosed type 1 diabetes
- Certain medications, including SGLT2 inhibitors (which can occasionally cause DKA even when blood sugar is only moderately elevated)

Early warning signs
- High blood glucose (often above 200 to 250 mg/dL)
- High ketone levels in blood or urine
- Extreme thirst and frequent urination
- Fatigue and dry mouth

Emergency symptoms requiring immediate medical care
Seek emergency medical help right away if you notice any of the following:
- Nausea, vomiting, or inability to keep fluids down
- Abdominal (stomach) pain
- Fruity-smelling breath
- Rapid, deep breathing or shortness of breath
- Confusion, drowsiness, or difficulty staying awake
- Blood ketone levels of 1.6 mmol/L or higher (or moderate-to-large urine ketones)

Safety steps for high blood sugar and ketones
- Check for ketones whenever you feel sick or your blood glucose rises above 240 mg/dL.
- Do NOT exercise if ketones are present and your blood sugar is high, because physical activity can push blood sugar and ketone levels even higher.
- Drink plenty of sugar-free fluids (such as water) to prevent dehydration, follow your health care provider's sick-day plan, and contact your doctor or emergency services promptly if ketones are elevated or symptoms worsen.
