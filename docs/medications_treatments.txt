Insulin, Medicines, & Other Diabetes Treatments

Overview
Alongside healthy eating and regular physical activity, medications play a key role in managing diabetes. The specific medicine plan depends on the type of diabetes, blood sugar patterns, other health conditions, and personal preferences. Always consult a licensed health care provider before starting, changing, or stopping any medication.

Insulin therapy
People with type 1 diabetes must take insulin every day because their bodies no longer make it. Some people with type 2 diabetes or gestational diabetes also take insulin when other treatments are not enough to reach blood glucose targets.
Common ways to take insulin include:
- Syringes and vials
- Insulin pens (pre-filled or reusable cartridges)
- Insulin pumps (small wearable devices delivering steady background insulin plus mealtime doses)
- Automated insulin delivery systems (hybrid closed-loop / artificial pancreas systems pairing a CGM with an insulin pump)
- Inhaled rapid-acting insulin for some adults
Different types of insulin—rapid-acting, short-acting, intermediate-acting, and long-acting—differ in how quickly they start working and how long they last.

Oral and non-insulin injectable medicines for type 2 diabetes
Many people with type 2 diabetes manage blood sugar with oral pills or non-insulin injections:
- Metformin: Often the first medication prescribed for type 2 diabetes. It lowers the amount of glucose released by the liver and helps the body respond better to insulin.
- Other medication classes (such as GLP-1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, sulfonylureas, and thiazolidinediones) work in different ways—helping the pancreas release insulin, slowing digestion, or removing excess glucose through urine. Some newer classes also offer heart and kidney protection.

Working with your care team
Take medications as prescribed, learn whether any of your medicines can cause low blood sugar (hypoglycemia), and attend regular follow-up visits to review A1C results and adjust treatment safely.
