Know Your Facts About Diabetes | American Diabetes Association
https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/diabetes-myths
Myth 1: Eating too much sugar causes diabetes.
Fact: Diabetes is not caused simply by eating sugar. Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition caused by genetics and unknown triggers. Type 2 diabetes is caused by a combination of genetics and lifestyle factors. However, drinking sugar-sweetened beverages—such as regular soda (a single 12-ounce can has about 150 calories and 40 grams of sugar), sweet tea, and fruit punch—contributes to weight gain and raises the risk of type 2 diabetes.

Myth 2: Only people who are overweight or have obesity get type 2 diabetes.
Fact: Being overweight is an important risk factor for type 2 diabetes, but other factors such as family history, age, ethnicity, and physical inactivity also play major roles. Many people at a healthy weight develop type 2 diabetes, and many people with excess weight never develop it.

Myth 3: Needing insulin for type 2 diabetes means you failed to manage your diabetes.
Fact: Type 2 diabetes is a progressive condition. Over time, the pancreas naturally produces less insulin, even when a person eats well and stays active. Adding insulin is a helpful, effective tool to keep blood glucose in a safe range—not a personal failure.

Myth 4: People with diabetes cannot eat fruit, bread, or any carbohydrates.
Fact: People with diabetes do not have to eliminate carbohydrates or fruit. Carbohydrates—especially whole grains, beans, vegetables, and whole fruits—can be part of a healthy meal plan when portions are balanced using tools like the Diabetes Plate Method.

Myth 5: Diabetes is not a serious disease.
Fact: Diabetes is a serious chronic condition that can lead to heart disease, stroke, kidney disease, vision loss, and nerve damage if left unmanaged. Fortunately, good blood sugar, blood pressure, and cholesterol management greatly lowers the risk of complications.

Myth 6: You can catch diabetes from someone else.
Fact: Diabetes is not contagious. It cannot be spread like a cold or the flu.

Myth 7: People with diabetes cannot exercise or play sports.
Fact: Regular physical activity is strongly encouraged for most people with diabetes because it improves insulin sensitivity, lowers blood sugar, and supports heart health. With proper planning and glucose monitoring, people with diabetes can participate in almost any sport.
