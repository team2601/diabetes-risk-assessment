Get Active | Diabetes | CDC

Key points
- Physical activity is one of the most effective tools for managing diabetes and preventing type 2 diabetes.
- Being active makes your body more sensitive to insulin, which helps lower blood sugar levels during and after exercise.

Recommended activity goals
- Aim for at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity—for example, 30 minutes a day, 5 days a week.
- Moderate-intensity activities include brisk walking, biking, swimming, water aerobics, mowing the lawn, and dancing. You should be able to talk, but not sing, while doing them.
- Try not to go more than 2 days in a row without being physically active so insulin sensitivity stays elevated.
- Add muscle-strengthening activities (such as lifting light weights, resistance bands, or bodyweight exercises) on at least 2 days each week.

Health benefits of regular physical activity
Regular movement helps you:
- Lower blood sugar levels and improve A1C over time
- Lower blood pressure and LDL ("bad") cholesterol
- Raise HDL ("good") cholesterol
- Maintain a healthy weight and reduce body fat
- Improve mood, sleep quality, balance, and energy levels
- Lower the risk of heart disease and nerve damage

How to get started safely
- Start small if you are new to exercise: begin with a 10-minute brisk walk after dinner and gradually increase duration and pace.
- Find activities you enjoy or walk with a friend or family member to stay consistent.
- Wear comfortable, well-fitting shoes and check your feet before and after exercise for blisters, redness, or sores.
- Stay hydrated before, during, and after activity.
- If you take insulin or medications that can lower blood sugar, check your blood sugar before and after exercise and carry a fast-acting carbohydrate snack in case of low blood sugar.
- Talk with your health care provider before starting a vigorous new exercise routine, especially if you have heart, eye, or nerve complications.
