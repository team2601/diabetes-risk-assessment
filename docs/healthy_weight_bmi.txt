Healthy Weight | Diabetes | CDC

Key points
- Carrying excess weight, particularly around the waist, increases insulin resistance and raises the risk of prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, and heart disease.
- Even modest weight loss of 5% to 7% of your current body weight improves blood sugar, blood pressure, and cholesterol.

Understanding Body Mass Index (BMI)
Body Mass Index (BMI) is a screening tool that estimates body weight relative to height (weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared, kg/m2). Standard adult BMI categories are:
- Underweight: Below 18.5
- Healthy weight: 18.5 to 24.9
- Overweight: 25.0 to 29.9
- Obesity: 30.0 or higher
Note: For people of Asian descent, diabetes risk can begin at a lower BMI (around 23.0 or higher). BMI is a general screening metric and should be evaluated alongside waist size and other health measurements.

Waist circumference and abdominal fat
Fat stored around the abdomen is strongly linked to insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes risk. Risk increases when waist circumference is:
- Greater than 35 inches for nonpregnant women
- Greater than 40 inches for men

Practical steps for sustainable weight management
- Aim for steady, sustainable weight loss (about 1 to 2 pounds per week) rather than restrictive crash diets.
- Use the Diabetes Plate Method: fill half your plate with non-starchy vegetables, one quarter with lean protein, and one quarter with whole-grain or high-fiber carbohydrates.
- Combine at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week with muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days per week.
- Track meals, portions, and physical activity, get enough sleep, and manage stress to avoid emotional eating.
