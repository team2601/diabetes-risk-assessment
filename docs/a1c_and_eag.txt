What is the A1C test?
The A1C test (also called HbA1c or glycated hemoglobin test) is a blood test that reflects your average blood glucose level over the past 2 to 3 months. It measures the percentage of hemoglobin—a protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen—that is coated with sugar. The higher your average blood sugar levels, the higher your A1C percentage.

A1C ranges for diagnosis
- Normal: Below 5.7%
- Prediabetes: 5.7% to 6.4%
- Diabetes: 6.5% or higher

A1C goals for people with diabetes
For most nonpregnant adults with diabetes, the American Diabetes Association recommends an A1C goal of 7% or lower. Individual targets may be higher or lower depending on age, pregnancy, other health conditions, and risk of low blood sugar—always confirm your personal target with your health care provider.

Converting A1C to Estimated Average Glucose (eAG)
Because daily blood glucose meters report blood sugar in mg/dL while A1C is reported as a percentage, the Estimated Average Glucose (eAG) translates A1C into the same units used on home meters. The relationship is based on the ADAG formula: eAG (mg/dL) = 28.7 x A1C - 46.7.

Official A1C to eAG conversion table:
- A1C 6.0% = 126 mg/dL
- A1C 6.5% = 140 mg/dL
- A1C 7.0% = 154 mg/dL
- A1C 7.5% = 169 mg/dL
- A1C 8.0% = 183 mg/dL
- A1C 8.5% = 197 mg/dL
- A1C 9.0% = 212 mg/dL
- A1C 9.5% = 226 mg/dL
- A1C 10.0% = 240 mg/dL

Why both A1C and daily checks matter
A1C gives a big-picture 2-to-3-month average, while daily fingerstick or continuous glucose monitor (CGM) checks reveal day-to-day highs and lows after meals, exercise, or medication changes.
