Key points
- Simple blood tests are used to diagnose prediabetes, type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes, and gestational diabetes.
- Ask a doctor to check your blood sugar if you have symptoms or risk factors, since prediabetes and type 2 diabetes often have no obvious warning signs.

Diagnostic blood tests and thresholds
1. HbA1c test (A1C)
The A1C test measures your average blood sugar level over the past 2 to 3 months. No fasting is required.
- Normal: Below 5.7%
- Prediabetes: 5.7% to 6.4%
- Diabetes: 6.5% or higher
Note: Certain conditions—such as severe anemia, kidney failure, liver disease, hemoglobin variants, or pregnancy—can affect A1C accuracy.

2. Fasting plasma glucose test (FPG)
This test measures your blood sugar after an overnight fast of at least 8 hours (no food or drink except water).
- Normal: 99 mg/dL or below (less than 100 mg/dL)
- Prediabetes: 100 to 125 mg/dL
- Diabetes: 126 mg/dL or higher

3. Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)
This test measures your blood sugar before and 2 hours after you drink a liquid containing glucose to see how your body processes sugar.
- Normal: Below 140 mg/dL
- Prediabetes: 140 to 199 mg/dL
- Diabetes: 200 mg/dL or higher

4. Random plasma glucose test
This test measures blood sugar at any time of day without fasting.
- Diabetes: 200 mg/dL or higher, particularly when paired with classic symptoms of high blood sugar.

Testing for type 1 diabetes
If a doctor suspects type 1 diabetes, additional tests may include:
- Autoantibody blood tests to check for immune markers associated with type 1 diabetes.
- Urine or blood ketone tests to check for diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA).

Testing for gestational diabetes
Pregnant women who have not previously been diagnosed with diabetes are typically screened between 24 and 28 weeks of pregnancy:
- Glucose screening test (1-hour): You drink a sugary liquid and have blood drawn 1 hour later. A result of 140 mg/dL or higher usually requires a follow-up oral glucose tolerance test to confirm gestational diabetes.
- Doctors may test earlier in pregnancy if you have strong risk factors.
